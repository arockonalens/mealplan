### Australian notes — Tim writes American, and two of these bite

- **"Unfiltered apple cider" means cloudy apple JUICE, not alcoholic cider.**
  This is the one that would actually ruin the braise here.
- **Diamond Crystal kosher salt**: his measurements assume it. With fine salt
  use about **half** the volume; with flaked salt, near enough 1:1.
- butternut squash = **butternut pumpkin** · lacinato kale = **cavolo nero**
- **Kodiak protein mix is not sold here** — the flour version is in his recipe.
- Farro is in the Coles health aisle and is dear; **pearl barley** is cheaper,
  always stocked, and behaves the same in a cold bowl.
- 425 °F = 220 °C · 400 = 200 · 375 = 190 · 325 = 165. 3 lb chicken = 1.35 kg,
  1 lb sausage = 450 g, 12 oz bacon = 340 g.
- **Seasonality:** this is an American autumn menu landing in a Melbourne
  late spring. The cold farro bowl travels perfectly. A long braise and a
  baked cream pasta are heavy for a warm week — worth weighing before both
  go in the same seven days.

## 2026-11-08 — Greg's own TikTok: velveting (@goosetheory05)

Sent mid-build on 8 Nov: <https://vt.tiktok.com/ZSpkcCbx6/> — "Velveting Beef
Recipe: Silky, Succulent Mongolian Beef at Home". **Shipped the same day** as
Sunday dinner, at 460 g, rescaled when Greg weighed his steaks, because he had two steaks.

The technique is the valuable part and it is now banked (see below). The
dish itself is used — do not re-propose Mongolian beef — but **velveting
applies to any cheap, tough cut** and has not been used on anything else:
chicken thigh, rump, blade, round, even prawns.

**The caveat to keep saying:** velveting is a *rescue* for tough cuts. On a
good steak it is wasted and a hot pan would do better. Say so rather than
applying it everywhere because it is new.

## Techniques banked (already in use)

- **The oven only goes down** — set it once at the top temperature and
  descend. Used in the 1–7 Nov cook-up (220 → 200 → 180).
- **A slow-cooker dish keeps the oven free** all afternoon.
- **One pot of pasta water, twice** — lift the first batch out with a
  strainer, drop the second in. Not yet used.
- **Twenty minutes of nothing but chopping** before any heat. Not yet used.
- **Velveting** (8 Nov, @goosetheory05) — bicarb at 3 tsp a kilo through raw sliced meat,
  40 min, **rinsed off and dried**, then cornflour + egg white + soy, then a
  second dry cornflour dusting, flash-fried at **120–140 °C**. The rinse and
  the second dusting are the two steps people skip. Works on any tough cut.
- **One roast, split two ways** (14 Nov, Tim Laielli) — roast a single big
  tray of vegetable, then send half into a cold lunch bowl and half into a hot
  dinner. One oven slot, two dishes. **Directly cuts Sunday hands-on time**,
  which is the number Greg watches.
- **Fruit compote** — diced fruit, butter, maple, cardamom and cinnamon, 8–10
  minutes until syrupy but still holding shape, then lemon juice off the heat.
- **Lemon-tahini** — it seizes solid when you whisk the acid in. **Keep going
  with ice water a tablespoon at a time** and it comes back pourable.
- **Kale, massaged** — a pinch of salt and a squeeze of lemon, one minute, until
  it darkens. Raw kale in a make-ahead bowl needs this or it stays leathery.
- **Roast vegetable blended into a pasta sauce** — stock, a little cream,
  parmesan, nutmeg. Carries the sauce without the sauce being cream.
