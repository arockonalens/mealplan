Chest three times a week, never on consecutive days — Monday heavy, Wednesday light and upper, Saturday lower. Muscle protein synthesis runs about 48 hours, so three hits a week is roughly the ceiling on how often a muscle can usefully be trained, and every gap here is at least 48 hours. The three sessions are deliberately different: if they were the same workout done three times you would just get tired. Arms lose their day. Wednesday was arms and is now chest. That is the real cost of this and you should know it: pressing three times a week already hammers the triceps, and the two back sessions cover the biceps, so nothing is abandoned — but neither is trained directly any more. The Arms tab is still there if you want to bolt fifteen minutes of it onto Thursday. Thursday itself is unchanged, shoulders then abs. Back stays twice, Tuesday and the back half of Saturday, and that is not negotiable: all this pressing with no pulling rolls the shoulders forward, which hurts first and stops you pressing second. Friday and Sunday are real rest days.

01

Incline Chest Press

Technogym Selection Pro · incline chest press

Technogym Chest Press — Selection 700 or 900

Seated, upright back pad, two independent lever arms with handles beside your chest, weight stack behind you. Most Technogym floors have no dedicated incline machine: use the standard chest press with the seat dropped to its lowest setting, which raises the handles relative to your chest and gives you the angle. Failing that, an adjustable bench at 30° with dumbbells in the Pure area.

4sets6–8reps120srest3-1-1tempo
  • Set the seat so the handles sit at upper-chest height, not shoulder height. Too high and it becomes a shoulder press.
  • Press up and slightly in. Stop just short of locking the elbows — the tension is the point, not the lockout.
  • Upper chest is the part almost everyone under-builds, which is why it goes first while you are fresh.
  • Last two reps should be genuinely hard with form intact. If rep 10 is easy, the weight goes up next session.

Technogym Selection Assisted Chin / Dip

Tall frame, pull-up bar on top, dip bars at waist height, and a padded platform for your knees or feet. The stack takes weight off you rather than adding it, so a higher number is more help, not less — that is backwards from every other machine here and it catches everyone out once.

04

Pectoral Machine

Technogym Selection Pro · pectoral machine (pec deck)

Technogym Pectoral Machine — often a combined Pectoral / Rear Delt

The giveaway is the arms: vertical pads you press with your forearms, not handles you push with your hands. If it has handles pointing forward you are at the chest press. If it is the combined pec/rear-delt version, the arms have to be swung round to the front position before you sit down.

3sets12–15reps60srest2-1-2tempo

  • Squeeze for a full second where the pads meet. This is the only point in the session where the chest is fully shortened.
  • Lighter than feels impressive. This is a stretch-and-squeeze exercise, not a load exercise.

Rope on the high pulley

05

Pallof Press

Technogym Dual Adjustable Pulley · handle at chest height

Stand side-on to the column, feet shoulder width, handle clasped in both hands at the sternum. Step away until the cable is properly loaded.

3sets10–12 each sidereps45srest2-1-2tempo
  • The cable is trying to twist you towards the machine. The entire exercise is refusing to let it. Nothing about your torso should move.
  • Press straight out and back in. The further the handle gets from your chest, the harder the cable pulls — that is the rep.
  • Same job as a plank, bracing the core against a force, except you can add weight to it. A plank you can only make longer, which stops being training and starts being time.
  • Ribs down, glutes tight, breathe out as you press. If your shoulders rotate at all, drop a plate.

Sunday cook-up

60 minutes · nothing is baked, and the beef is dinner rather than a batch

60 minutes hands-on, down from 95, and the shortest Sunday this plan has had. Two reasons: neither snack is baked, and the beef is one dinner rather than a batch — two steaks, cooked and eaten tonight. So there is no oven ladder; the oven goes on once at 200 °C for a single tray and then off. The velveting sets the clock, because 40 minutes of bicarb and 30 of marinade are both waiting rather than working, and the chicken tray and the parfait jar fit inside that waiting. Method from @goosetheory05 on TikTok, used at its own 500 g scale.

  1. 12:00 — slice the steaks, bicarb them, walk away

    Sliced thin against the grain. A generous teaspoon and a half of bicarb worked through, then 40 minutes in the fridge. This is the velveting, and it is the only step here that cannot be hurried.

  2. 12:05 — oven to 200, chicken and sweet potato in

    Breast butterflied flat, sweet potato in 2 cm cubes, one tray. The oven is used once today and then switched off.

  3. 12:15 — the parfait jar, while both things sit

    Pecans toasted dry and watched. Chopped, then oats and cinnamon stirred in and the lot into a jar — that is the whole sweet snack for the week. Bowl dressing shaken in a second jar: Dijon, honey, vinegar, oil.

  4. 12:30 — chicken out at 70 °C, rested, oven off

    Five minutes resting, then sliced. Four portions to the fridge, two to the freezer for Friday.

  5. 12:40 — beef rinsed, dried, marinated

    Rinse the bicarb off properly or it tastes of soap. Then cornflour, egg white, soy, white pepper and a spoon of oil through it. Thirty minutes.

  6. 12:45 — wings rubbed, salmon split, grinders bagged

    Wings dried hard, tossed with lemon pepper and baking powder, back in the fridge raw; half of them into the freezer for Thursday and Saturday. Salmon into three 400 g lots, one of them frozen. Grinder meat and cheese bagged in three, Saturday's to the freezer.

  7. 1:15 — fry the beef and eat

    Second dusting of dry cornflour, excess shaken off. Oil to 120–140 °C — cooler than you would guess. One batch is plenty at 500 g. Then capsicum, ginger, garlic, the sauce, and the whole bunch of spring onion through it. Rice on while you fry.

The bare minimum, if you only have half an hour. Bicarb the steaks and put the chicken tray in. Everything else here — the wing rub, the salmon split, the grinder bags, the parfait jar — is ten minutes of bagging that can happen any evening. Nothing this week needs a long cook, which is the trade for the beef not stretching past tonight.

What moves where

The freezer carries this week — three of the four batch mains use it

Nothing cooked goes past three days in the fridge without being frozen, and with only four batch mains covering thirteen meals, three of them run past that. The wings are the important one: they are frozen raw, not cooked, so Thursday and Saturday are still fried from scratch.

  1. Tonight — the beef, and that is it

    Two portions, both eaten. There are no leftovers from a velveted stir-fry and there is no point pretending otherwise — the coating goes soft and the beef goes grey.

  2. Sunday night — wings into two lots, still raw

    Half to the fridge for Monday and Tuesday. Half frozen raw, out Wednesday night and again Friday night. Raw chicken does not make day six in a fridge.

  3. Sunday night — salmon into three 400 g lots

    Monday and Wednesday to the fridge. Friday's frozen, out Thursday night. Buy it fresh and freeze it the same day, not on Wednesday.

  4. Sunday night — harvest chicken into two lots

    Four portions to the fridge for Sunday and Wednesday. Two frozen, out Thursday night for Friday lunch. Apple and dressing are added on the day either way.

  5. Sunday night — grinder meat bagged in three

    Tuesday and Thursday to the fridge, Saturday's bag to the freezer. Ham and salami both freeze fine; the salad is made fresh twice and never kept past two days.

  6. Nothing to do — the edamame

    Stays in its bags in the freezer all week. Five minutes from frozen, as you want it. This is the one thing here you cannot get wrong.

  7. The two jars in the fridge door

    The bowl dressing and the nut-and-oat mix. Both outlast the week; neither needs remaking.

The rule underneath all of this: cooked dishes get eaten inside about three days or they get frozen, and anything frozen thaws in the fridge overnight rather than on the bench. The salmon is the one to watch — it is the only main here with no margin at all. For actual times and temperatures, Food Standards Australia New Zealand rather than a meal plan.
