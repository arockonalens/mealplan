Sunday, in order

≈2 hr elapsed · ~45 min hands-on

The slow cooker does the chicken unattended, the dough proves while you work, and the oven runs two jobs back to back. Hands-on time is about 50 minutes spread across the afternoon.

  1. Thigh fillets into the slow cooker

    Harissa rubbed through, onion and lemon underneath, no liquid needed. Low for 6 hours. First thing, then forget about it — this is Monday and Tuesday sorted while the oven does tonight.

  2. Mix the focaccia dough

    Ten minutes in the mixer with the dough hook, then it proves on its own for 2–3 hours while everything else happens.

  3. Boil six eggs and make the salad mix every week

    Twelve minutes, straight into cold water. Mixed with yoghurt, smoked paprika and lemon while still warm — it takes the seasoning better. Today’s lunch and tomorrow’s sandwiches come out of this one bowl.

  4. Slaw base and spice paste — food processor

    Cabbage and carrots shredded in thirty seconds, stored dry. Then blitz your own harissa: roasted capsicum, chilli, garlic, cumin, oil. Far better than the jar and a fraction of the price.

  5. Cook 500 g rice

    Spread on a tray to cool fast. Saturday’s fried rice needs it properly cold.

  6. Oven 180 °C — bake the oat slice

    25–30 min. Cool completely in the tin before cutting.

  7. Oven up to 220 °C — dimple and bake the focaccia

    20–25 min until deep gold. Last in, because it wants the hottest oven.

  8. Sear the beef and get the braise in

    Oven at 165 °C for two hours, then the leek stage. It shares the oven with the brownie and focaccia if you stagger them. Shred the thigh when the slow cooker is done.

No lentils. Chickpeas are fine and worth knowing about: dried, they are $0.030 per gram of protein — less than half what eggs or tuna cost and the cheapest thing in the shop by a distance. Not in this week’s list, but a $3 bag is the single best value add there is.

Freezer plan

One shop, seven days — nothing sits too long

Raw chicken should not be sitting in the fridge from Sunday to Wednesday, and cooked meat is a three-day proposition. This is what moves where, and when.

  1. Sunday, unpacking the bags

    Chicken breast straight to the freezer — it is not needed until Wednesday. Thigh fillets and beef chuck to the fridge; both get cooked within 24 hours.

  2. Sunday, after cooking

    Half the focaccia sliced and frozen. Half the pulled thigh into the freezer for Wednesday’s melt; the rest stays in the fridge for Tuesday.

  3. Sunday night, after dinner

    Friday’s half of the braise and its strained liquid straight into the freezer. Left in the fridge it would be five days old by Friday.

  4. Tuesday night

    Wednesday’s pulled thigh out of the freezer into the fridge.

  5. Wednesday, while the slow cooker runs

    Bake the frittata with whatever the fridge has accumulated plus the ham. Cool, cut into six, fridge. That is the savoury snack from Thursday to Saturday.

  6. Wednesday night

    Portion Saturday’s share of the cooked breast into the freezer. Left in the fridge it would be four days old by Saturday, which is past where I would eat it.

  7. Thursday night

    The braise out of the freezer into the fridge for Friday’s ragu, and the last of the focaccia with it.

The rule underneath all of this: cooked dishes get eaten within about three days or they get frozen, and anything frozen thaws in the fridge overnight rather than on the bench. For actual times and temperatures, Food Standards Australia New Zealand rather than a meal plan.

  • Three days, then freeze. This plan assumes cooked dishes get eaten within about three days. That's deliberately conservative — portion and freeze anything past it rather than pushing your luck.
  • Cool fast, then fridge. Shallow containers, spread out. A full hot pot straight in warms everything around it.
  • Rice needs care. Cool it quickly, refrigerate promptly, reheat once only until steaming. That's why Saturday's fried rice wants properly chilled rice.
